HTC2022
=======

**Measured** fan-beam CT data of 26 acrylic phantoms, 7 cm discs with holes
of different shapes, made for limited-angle CT: the data of the Helsinki
Tomography Challenge 2022 (Meaney, Silva de Moura, Juvonen and Siltanen,
Finnish Inverse Problems Society; Zenodo, version 1.4.0, CC BY 4.0). Each
phantom was scanned over a full circle in 0.5° steps; the challenge gave arcs
of it, from 90° (level 1) down to 30° (level 7).

=================  ===========================================================
Training           5 phantoms (4 with holes, 1 solid disc)
Test               21 phantoms, 7 levels × 3, with more and more complex holes
Sinograms          720 angles × 560 pixels (0.2 mm, binned 4×)
Images             512 × 512 pixels of 0.148 mm
Geometry           fan beam, 410.66 mm source to origin, 553.74 mm to detector
Size               124 MB
=================  ===========================================================

Download and prepare
--------------------

.. code-block:: bash

   python LION/data_loaders/HTC2022/download.py
   python LION/data_loaders/HTC2022/pre_process_HTC2022.py
   python LION/data_loaders/HTC2022/validate_HTC2022.py

The first writes ``LION_DATA_PATH/raw/HTC2022``, the second
``LION_DATA_PATH/processed/HTC2022`` (it needs a GPU), the third checks the
second against the challenge's files (below).

What the preparation does
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The test phantoms' arcs start at different angles, but a LION dataset has one
geometry. As each scan is a full circle in 0.5° steps, shifting a phantom's
sinogram cyclically turns the phantom exactly: the preparation shifts each so
that its challenge arc starts at the first angle. The same geometry, with the
first 181 angles, then gives every level-1 arc, with the first 61 every
level-7 one.

The targets are made again from the turned full sinograms, as the challenge
made its own: the FBP (LION's FDK), and its segmentation (negative values set
to 0, Otsu's threshold, then a closing with a disc of radius 3 pixels;
``HTC2022.segment``). The challenge's files are kept, as released, next to
them.

``validate_HTC2022.py`` checks, for each phantom: that the challenge's
limited-angle sinogram is the turned sinogram's first rows; that LION's FBP
and segmentation of the full scan, not turned, match the challenge's
(correlation; the challenge's score, the Matthews correlation coefficient),
and in which orientation; that the targets match the challenge's turned by
the same angle (approximate: interpolated); and that the operator projects
the reconstructions to their sinograms. It writes a table and a figure.

In LION
-------

The loader is ``LION.data_loaders.HTC2022.HTC2022``: "train" (the 5 training
phantoms) and "test" (the 21) splits; there is no validation split. A sample
is (sinogram, target). Its parameters (``HTC2022.default_parameters()``)
choose the target, "reconstruction" (the FBP) or "segmentation" (the
challenge's ground truth), and the geometry: ``HTC2022.geometry(angles)``, the
first ``angles`` angles of the scan (all 720 by default).

.. code-block:: python

   from LION.data_loaders.HTC2022 import HTC2022, geometry

   # level 1: a 90 degree arc (181 angles), to the segmentation
   parameters = HTC2022.default_parameters()
   parameters.target = "segmentation"
   test = HTC2022("test", geometry(181), parameters)
   sinogram, segmentation = test[0]
   print(test.sample_dataframe)  # each phantom's level and arc

With 5 training phantoms, it is above all a test set of measured data, e.g.
for methods trained on simulated data.

.. note::

   The challenge scored segmentations of its 21 test phantoms, each from its
   own arc. Here each phantom is turned so its arc starts at the first angle,
   and the targets are LION's FBP and segmentation of the turned full scan:
   ``validate_HTC2022.py`` measures how close they are to the challenge's.
